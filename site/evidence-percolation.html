Evidence percolation

A theoretical proposal, reformulated after adversarial review. The knowledge layer behind this project grades every claim on a graduated epistemic scale rather than a binary fact/inference split. This page asks whether the promotion of a claim up that scale behaves like a critical transition on a graph of evidence — isolated below a threshold amount of independent corroboration, abruptly promoted above it — and writes down the mathematics that would make the analogy precise. Two corrections carry the argument: the graph's super-criticality is the Molloy–Reed second-moment ratio κ, not the Erdős–Rényi ⟨k⟩=1 connectivity threshold; and promotion — which needs several convergent paths, not one — is a threshold cascade (bootstrap / k-core), not single-edge connectivity. The percolation and random-graph results are established and cited; the mapping onto epistemic status is a working hypothesis, offered to be tested, not a finding.

The graduated scale this formalises

The project's knowledge layer does not label a claim fact or inference. It places the claim on a six-rung ladder, and promotion up the ladder is gated by the amount of independent corroboration accumulated — not by how confident the claim reads.8

  • 1speculative_hypothesis — a conceptual claim, no empirical support yet (an idea, an analogy, a single anecdote).
  • 2working_hypothesis — supported by pilot data or indirect/analogous literature; one credible source.
  • 3corroborated_inference — multiple independent sources or analyses converge. This rung is where the transition sits.
  • 4operational_fact — embedded in a defined, auditable workflow; relied upon in practice.
  • 5canonical_fact — guideline-, textbook-, or strong-consensus-level.
  • ×contradicted — a sink, not a rung: a claim lands here from any level when opposing evidence outweighs its support.

The existing promotion rule scores a claim by summing weighted, independence-discounted evidence and promoting when the score clears a threshold, subject to a mandatory contradiction check.8 The proposal below is that this score is a local proxy for a corroboration branching process — and that promotion resembles crossing a percolation threshold, with the caveats made explicit there.

Percolation: the established scaffold

The following results are standard percolation and random-graph theory. They are presented as known and cited; the mapping to evidence comes afterwards.

The percolation transition

Broadbent and Hammersley1 introduced bond percolation and the sharp-threshold phenomenon. For an infinite graph with edges open independently with probability p, let θ(p) be the probability that a given vertex lies in an infinite open cluster C(v). There is a critical probability pc with

established Below pc only finite clusters exist almost surely; above it an infinite (giant) cluster appears. The transition is sharp.1,2

On a Bethe lattice (Cayley tree) of coordination number z, the threshold is exact:2

established Each non-root node has z−1 forward branches; the cluster survives when the expected number of open forward branches, (z−1)p, reaches 1. This branching-ratio-equals-one criterion governs connectivity — whether a giant support component exists at all. It is not the criterion for claim promotion: promotion demands several convergent paths, which is a threshold cascade with a different, discontinuous transition (see “The core correction” below).

Order parameter and critical exponent

The percolation strength (order parameter) P∞(p) — the fraction of the graph in the giant cluster — rises continuously from zero at threshold as a power law:2

established Near threshold the order parameter grows as (p−pc)β. On trees and in the mean-field universality class the exponent is β=12 — the class the proposal assumes a sparse evidence graph sits in.

Giant component on a random graph

For the Erdős–Rényi graph G(n,p), a unique giant component emerges exactly when the mean degree ⟨k⟩ passes 1; its size fraction S solves a self-consistency equation:3,6

established The threshold ⟨k⟩=1 is the Erdős–Rényi giant-component birth;3 S is the survival probability of a Poisson branching process with mean ⟨k⟩.5 This homogeneous ⟨k⟩=1 is only the Poisson (ER) special case; for a heterogeneous graph the general condition is Molloy–Reed (Eq. 7), so ⟨k⟩≈1 must not be read as the general threshold.

For a graph with an arbitrary degree distribution pk (a configuration model), the Molloy–Reed criterion gives the giant-component condition:4

established Real corroboration is heterogeneous — some claims attract many attestations, most few — so the relevant threshold is Molloy–Reed, not the homogeneous ⟨k⟩=1. Writing the second-moment ratio κ≡⟨k2⟩/⟨k⟩, a giant component exists when κ>2, and the critical fraction of random node removal is qc=1−1/(κ−1).4,7 Degree-two branching is the boundary. Measured on this project's own wikilink graph, κ≈31.9 — deeply super-critical (for that value qc≈0.97 as a null-model expectation, i.e. tolerant of ~97% random node loss). κ is a structural / control parameter, not an order parameter — the order parameter is the giant-component fraction. Caveat: how κ was computed (which edges, which snapshot) is to be documented reproducibly, and on a finite graph the transition is blurred rather than sharp.

Divergent cluster size at the transition

Approaching threshold from below, the mean size of the cluster containing a random vertex (the percolation susceptibility χ) diverges:2

established Just below threshold, adding one more independent link can join large sub-clusters and enlarge the support cluster disproportionately — the susceptibility spike. This is the formal counterpart of "one good independent replication tips it".

Mapping percolation to graduated epistemic status

Speculative Everything from here is the proposed map. It reinterprets the project's existing evidence score as a branching ratio and reads epistemic status off the percolation order parameter. It is a conjecture to structure thinking and generate tests — not an empirical claim, and not fitted to any dataset.

Three quantities must be kept apart. The project's evidence score at a single claim — its weighted, independence-discounted local support, written μ(h)8 — is a node-level quantity: a useful ranking signal, not a criticality parameter. Whether a corroborating giant component exists at all is a global property of the whole graph. And a given claim's chance of belonging to it is a third, per-claim quantity. Written out:

proposed Keep the three apart. μ(h) is a local heuristic proxy — a node-level score we rank on (μ(h)≳θpromotion), not a criticality parameter. Criticality is global: a corroborating giant component exists only when the Perron root ρ(M) of the graph's next-generation (branching) matrix exceeds 1 — a property of the whole graph, not of any one claim. A claim's own standing is sh, the message-passing (cavity / belief-propagation) probability that h joins a large corroborating component; sh, not μ>1, is the right per-claim quantity. The μ(h)>1⟺ giant reading would conflate a local score with a global parameter; μ(h) survives only as a computable proxy for sh.8

The load-bearing assumption. This whole picture assumes corroboration is transitive along support edges — that support percolates hop to hop. But corroboration is intuitively local (many independent, reliable sources at the claim), while percolation criticality is global, and the transitivity is not obvious: B supporting A and C supporting B does not entail that C corroborates A. That transitivity is a modelling assumption, not a theorem — the central open question of the proposal.

Two rungs are not mere P∞ bands: operational_fact additionally requires embedding in an auditable workflow (and, in strict mode, human sign-off), and canonical_fact requires guideline support — gates the order parameter alone cannot express.8 The whole map is contingent on passing the contradiction gate, Eq. The qualitative content: speculative → working is sub-critical growth; the working → corroborated promotion is the critical transition, where the claim moves from an isolated finite cluster into the corroborating component; corroborated → operational → canonical is the further rise toward consensus. But the promotion rule needs several convergent paths, not one — and that changes the physics: it is not the ordinary, continuous (second-order) giant-component transition at ⟨k⟩=1 but a threshold cascade, whose transition is discontinuous. The next section makes this correction precise; it is why promotion feels like a phase change rather than a slider.

The core correction: promotion is a threshold cascade, not connectivity

Correction The reading above maps promotion onto ordinary percolation — a claim joins the giant component through a single connecting edge, a continuous (second-order) transition at ⟨k⟩=1. That is the wrong universality class for this rule. The promotion rule requires several convergent, provenance-distinct paths — a single long path is not proof — and requiring multiple corroborating neighbours is a different object: a threshold cascade, i.e. bootstrap percolation with activation threshold r≥2, equivalently k-core percolation for k≥3. This family has its own critical value, distinct from ⟨k⟩=1, and a discontinuous (first-order / hybrid) transition, categorically unlike the continuous giant-component birth.10,11 Bootstrap with r≥2 behaves qualitatively unlike the r=1 case that collapses to ordinary connectivity.9 So ⟨k⟩=1 is not the critical point for claim promotion.

The concrete model that fits is heterogeneous bootstrap percolation — each claim carries its own activation threshold, computed on its neighbours — which is provably equivalent to the Watts threshold model and appears under-studied in its own right.12 It fits this project's rule better than plain Watts because the rule is count-based (≥k distinct paths) rather than fraction-based (a fraction of active neighbours ≥ threshold).12,13 Caveats, from adversarial verification: the sharp jump is an N→∞ asymptote — on the finite vault expect a small-but-nonzero threshold and a blurred jump; and while the threshold-cascade family is the right home, no specific critical-seed formula is asserted here (several stronger quantitative claims — an all-or-nothing bootstrap bistability, an explicit critical-seed formula, and a three-regime directed-scale-free split — were refuted under verification and are deliberately omitted).

Falsifiable predictions and where this sits in the literature

The reformulation is testable. Two predictions separate it from ordinary percolation and from a flat graph-RAG baseline:

  • P1 — resilience is asymmetric. If the wikilink graph is scale-free with degree exponent 2<λ<3, the second moment ⟨k2⟩ diverges and the connectivity threshold vanishes: the giant component survives any finite fraction of random node removal but is fragile to targeted removal of hubs (the MOC notes).14 Measure: empirical node-removal curves, random vs targeted, and compare where the giant component breaks.
  • P2 — a discontinuity signature. The threshold-cascade formulation predicts an abrupt, hysteretic jump in the size of the promoted-claim set at a critical seed fraction — a signature distinguishable from the smooth giant-component growth of ordinary percolation.10,11 Measure: promoted-set size as a function of seed fraction; look for discontinuity / hysteresis versus smoothness (blurred, not sharp, on a finite graph).

Prior art. A full-text reading of the state-of-the-art GraphRAG survey finds no use of percolation theory — no giant component, k-core, bootstrap, cascade / threshold model, criticality, or phase transition; graph retrieval is framed as classical traversal (BFS/DFS, PageRank), and the exponentially expanding neighbourhood — the very object of a branching process — is treated as noise to prune, not a transition to formalise.15 The bridge from percolation theory to evidence aggregation in RAG is therefore a genuine gap. But the absence is also a caution: no one has needed this bridge yet, so the burden is on a measured retrieval delta of the percolation layer over a strong graph-RAG baseline. Absent that delta, “percolation-RAG” is framing, not a result.

Asymmetric risk: a higher threshold for clinical claims

For high-stakes and clinical claims the cost of wrongly promoting a shaky claim to "fact" far exceeds the cost of leaving a true claim under-graded.8 In percolation terms this raises the bar: a clinical claim must not merely cross threshold, it must cross it with a margin — the giant component must be robust, not marginal. Contradiction: anti-bonds and competing clusters

A contradicting edge is not the absence of support; it is negative support — an anti-bond. Split each claim's incident evidence into corroborating (μ+) and contradicting (μ−) branching ratios and net them:

proposed Anti-bonds subtract from the net local support and can collapse the claim's connection probability sh — de-percolation. When the opposing evidence dominates — its competing component outgrowing the corroborating one — h is displaced into the contradicted sink from any prior rung, as the framework requires.8 The check is mandatory before any promotion, so a contradiction is tested at every step, not only at the top.

Roadmap: the four shapes of knowledge

Evidence percolation is not a retrieval engine on its own — it is a promotion rule that sits on top of retrieval. The retrieval layer follows the project's core principle: knowledge comes in four shapes, and no single vector database serves all four.8 The working core today is a hybrid of a vector index plus a BM25 lexical index, fused by reciprocal rank fusion (see Architecture); the entity-relation graph — an emergent wikilink graph, decided 2026-07-04 — is planned as the next arm. The remaining primitives are planned, each matched to a shape the core does not cover, then orchestrated.

What this is and is not

This page is a theoretical proposal that recasts an existing, operational evidence-grading rule in the language of percolation. Its value, if any, is that it makes the rule's threshold behaviour predictable and its parameters interpretable — the branching-ratio criterion explains why promotion is abrupt, the susceptibility divergence explains why one independent replication can tip a claim, and the anti-bond formulation makes contradiction a first-class force rather than an afterthought. None of that is evidence that the map is correct. It would be tested by checking whether real promotion histories in the knowledge base show a sharp transition in cluster size at a reproducible corroboration threshold, and whether clinical claims sit systematically further above it. Until then it stays where it started: speculative.
